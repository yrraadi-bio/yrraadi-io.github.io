How do we know who to choose?

Data engine

By mapping tumor biology in real patient tissue.

Every tumor we profile adds one fully measured sample to the same reference, with its clinical annotations attached. With enough samples, a new tumor can be compared against the ones already measured, and the treatment outcomes recorded for the closest matches give a testable prediction for it.

Who it is for

Which is how you know who to choose.

The predicted map is read for the programmes a drug acts on, and the tumor is called against the outcomes already recorded for the tumors nearest it. Run that across everyone screened and a population that shares one diagnosis separates into the patients a drug is predicted to work in and the patients it is not.

Optimized trials cost less and reach more patients.

Bringing one cancer drug to market costs a median of $2.8 billion[1]. Most of the people who take the drugs below do not respond to them, and every percentage here is already measured inside the eligible group.

Predicting response before enrollment moves both numbers. A trial run on predicted responders is a smaller trial with a larger effect size, which is the largest single lever on what a drug costs to develop. And the patients it screens out stop being noise and become populations: distinct subgroups, each with the biology some other drug acts on, each one more people treated and a market that does not exist today.

  1. Keytruda

    pembrolizumab · anti–PD-1

    Biologic · IgG4 monoclonal antibody
    Marker · mismatch-repair deficiency

    34.3%[2]

    of eligible patients with MSI-H/dMMR solid tumors

    Objective response · KEYNOTE-158

    The first tissue-agnostic approval in oncology. Eligibility is read from a molecular test rather than from the organ the tumor started in, and two thirds of the patients it admits do not respond.

    $31.7B

    Revenue, 2025

  2. Opdivo

    nivolumab · anti–PD-1

    Biologic · IgG4 monoclonal antibody
    Marker · PD-L1 expression

    19.7%[3]

    of eligible patients with previously treated NSCLC

    Objective response · CheckMate 017 and 057

    Four in five treated patients do not respond, and the marker does not identify them: the trials reported that PD-L1 expression was neither prognostic nor predictive of benefit.

    $10.0B

    Revenue, 2025

  3. Cabometyx

    cabozantinib · multi-kinase inhibitor

    Small molecule · VEGFR2, MET and AXL inhibitor
    Marker · none established

    17%[4]

    of eligible patients with advanced renal cell carcinoma

    Objective response · METEOR

    Renal cancer has no marker that predicts response to a kinase inhibitor, so every patient is treated as though they might be the one in six whose tumor regresses, and most of them are not.

    $2.9B

    Revenue, 2025

  4. Tecentriq

    atezolizumab · anti–PD-L1

    Biologic · IgG1 monoclonal antibody
    Marker · PD-L1 expression

    14%[5]

    of eligible patients with previously treated NSCLC

    Objective response · OAK

    Fourteen percent responded, against 13 percent on the chemotherapy arm it was measured against. It earned its approval on how long the responses lasted rather than how often they came, and the survival benefit reached even patients whose tumors expressed no detectable PD-L1.

    $4.3B

    Revenue, 2025

  5. Yervoy

    ipilimumab · anti–CTLA-4

    Biologic · IgG1 monoclonal antibody
    Marker · none established

    10.9%[6]

    of eligible patients with previously treated melanoma

    Best overall response · MDX010-20

    The drug that opened checkpoint blockade, and it shrank tumors in one patient in nine. It was approved on survival rather than response, and there is still no test for who those nine are.

    $2.9B

    Revenue, 2025

  6. Erbitux

    cetuximab · anti–EGFR

    Biologic · IgG1 monoclonal antibody
    Marker · RAS wild-type, found after approval

    8.0%[7]

    of eligible patients with EGFR-expressing refractory colorectal cancer

    Partial response · CO.17

    Twenty-three of 287 patients responded, and the drug was approved on survival instead. Sorting those same tumors by KRAS status afterwards lifted response to 12.8% in the wild-type group and sank it to 1.2% in the mutant one[8], which is how RAS testing became the gate. It narrowed the eligible group and still leaves seven in eight of them with nothing.

    $1.3B

    Revenue, 2025

  7. Stivarga

    regorafenib · multi-kinase inhibitor

    Small molecule · VEGFR, KIT and RAF inhibitor
    Marker · none established

    1.0%[9]

    of eligible patients with treatment-refractory colorectal cancer

    Partial response · CORRECT

    Five tumors regressed out of 505, and median survival ran 1.4 months longer than placebo. Eligibility here is having exhausted everything else, which describes the patient rather than the tumor, so there is nothing in the criteria that could have found those five.

    $0.4B

    Revenue, 2025
